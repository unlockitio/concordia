Problem

Growing review volume needs a reconstructable decision trail

What goes wrong today
  • Manual review. Proposals and milestones are read case by case, under time pressure.
  • Implicit rules. Criteria live in people's heads, threads and changing documents.
  • Inconsistent application. Two reviewers, two readings of the same criterion.
  • Mutable evidence. A PR or issue can change after it was reviewed.
  • Hard to audit. "Why was this approved?" has no reconstructable answer.
Automation needs clear boundaries
  • Untrusted. An LLM that reads "the rules" from a moving document proves nothing.
  • Or too powerful. An agent that can approve or pay is an unaccountable authority.
  • No binding between rule and verdict. A verdict that cannot name the exact rule version it applied is an opinion.
Provable inputs connect the rules in force to the exact evidence judged.

Speaker notes

Frame the problem around evidence: reconstruct which requirements were applied to which exact artefact. Shared proof and versioned rules help reviewers explain decisions under growing volume. AI assessment supports human judgment while credentialed members retain decision authority.

Five PRIMARY seats plus two credentialed substitutes make seven factory credentials, not seven independent primary voters.
