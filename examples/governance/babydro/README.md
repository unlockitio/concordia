# BabyDRO: canton.name governance walkthrough

The CIPs credentials demo owns name requests, registry-issued credentials and their lifecycle. Concordia owns dispute governance and member entitlement only. `Registry.daml` defines a `Consortium` signed by the distinct DRO Party and **every current registry member**. The DRO creates a `GenesisProposal` fixing `(dro, consortiumId, founders, policy)`; each founder accepts via its own submit, adding its signature to the next proposal successor. Only after every named founder accepts can the DRO finalize the jointly signed consortium, without a joint `actAs` submit. Genesis unanimity is separate from the subsequent demo-local two-member approval threshold. The DRO is never a registry voter. The stable `consortiumId` and distinct DRO Party identify a chain but are not globally unique without contract keys. `MembershipMotion` and `PolicyMotion` are separate, independently lived templates, each bound to its originating Consortium CID, identity, electorate, payload, supporter set and root CID. They are never stored as a list on Consortium. A member proposes and a second member votes in separate transactions; consuming votes produce a successor of the same motion lineage. Enactment requires the original Consortium CID still to be active, so an unrelated governance successor makes an open motion stale; repropose against the latest successor. Admission additionally requires the newcomer's independently submitted invitation acceptance, before a member finalizes. A departing member is absent from the removal successor. `Dispute.daml` defines `NameDispute`, meaning a disagreement over two registries' claims to the same `canton.name`: `name` is the disputed name and `caseId` identifies this dispute. DRO-signed invitations, jointly signed ballots and DRO-signed rulings stay separate from credential state.

Opening a name dispute consumes `Consortium.OpenDispute` to check both disputing registries are members and two distinct active CIPs `Credential` interface IDs represent the same canonical demo name, with a single matching registry anchorer and the CIPs demo's party-subject structure. It creates the `NameDispute` and records its opening contract ID, frozen participants, name and credential IDs in the successor's authoritative active inventory. The non-submitting registry must disclose its credential for the opening fetch; disclosure grants visibility, not submitter authority. Governance successors carry that inventory forward. The DRO's signature is carried to the case. The first consuming ballot registration records the original case ID on its successor; later case successors preserve it. The DRO (outside the registry electorate) issues a case- and voter-scoped, pre-signed invitation; that voter independently exercises `DisputeInvitation_Accept` to create a jointly signed ballot and register its ID on the case. That ballot's voter independently exercises `Ballot_Cast` or `Ballot_Withdraw` to replace their ballot and update the case inventory atomically. These voter transactions do not exercise Consortium. A contract ID by itself is **not** authority: the invitation supplies the DRO's signature to the accepting voter. This follows the `BallotInvitation`/`PrivateBallot` delegation pattern in the private-majority-vote example. LF 2.2 lacks contract keys, so the caller must use the latest returned case and ballot IDs after each consuming choice; a stale ID fails. The case validates the voter and case identity of each ballot in its inventory at registration and resolution. The case-local `caseId` is not a global uniqueness guarantee across independently opened cases.

`SnapshotAtOpen` and `CurrentMembership` are demo-local policy options. The case freezes its opening policy and eligible voter set. `Consortium_Resolve` is the only resolution entry point: it verifies that the case's opening contract ID is in its own inventory, reads its own active membership, fetches the case's inventoried ballots, and applies the case's frozen policy. No caller-supplied membership set reaches the tally. A snapshot case still counts a removed *non-participant* voter's ballot, including one cast after removal. A current-membership case excludes that voter's ballot at tally; a later consortium policy change does not retarget the case. Dispute participants themselves cannot be removed while the case is active. Exercising a DRO-and-all-current-member-signed Consortium supplies its signatories' authority through the choice contract, but does **not** prove that the exercised contract is the unique live governance successor or that all signatories actively submitted the exercise. Direct creation requires the DRO and every named member, including for a competing Consortium; an existing jointly signed contract can carry its signatories' authority to successors under a member-controlled choice. The two-supporter registry governance threshold is therefore a business rule, not a claim of fresh unanimous transaction signatures. Only the DRO can issue a ruling, using the latest authoritative Consortium successor's membership and inventoried case. The `motionIsolation` script demonstrates two independently founded consortia; direct creation by all required signatories remains a possible colluding-signatories fork with copied inventory. This remains a colluding-signatories fork boundary, not an LF-level uniqueness guarantee. Because the DRO can sign a `NameDispute` directly and a successor's opening CID is an asserted field, matching the frozen inventory and requiring a live case do not cryptographically prove an unbroken ballot-successor lineage against a malicious DRO; a separately governed lineage mechanism would be needed for that stronger guarantee. The Consortium-carried active inventory freezes both participants even while independently signed case and ballot CIDs change. A member can propose and vote on removal, but `ApplyRemoval` rejects either participant in **any** active dispute; an unrelated member can still be removed under the normal threshold. After resolution, the entry is removed and a fresh removal motion can enact. There is no removal guard merely for an already resolved ruling. This does not establish global uniqueness across colluding-signatories forks.

The case's `HasCheckedFetch` instance groups disputes by DRO authority. Invitation acceptance checks that authority against the DRO-signed invitation, and resolution checks it against the Consortium's DRO before reading the inventoried case. The invitation still checks its token and case ID separately; checked fetch alone does not establish case provenance, current inventory or successor lineage. A demo rejects a same-token foreign-DRO case while accepting the invited case.

`Consortium_Resolve` takes the latest active `NameDispute` CID and a decision, never a caller-supplied credential CID. It requires two distinct eligible matching ballots and rejects a simultaneous two-vote opposing decision. Within one transaction it creates a signed `ArchiveClaimAction`, exercises Concordia `Action_AuthorizeExecution` to mint `ArchiveClaimExecution`, then exercises `Executable_Execute` on that execution, which archives the frozen loser via CIPs `Credential_ArchiveAsAnchorers`; it archives the temporary Action, closes the dispute and creates the ruling and a Consortium successor without that active entry. The Action fixes the losing CID as its target identity, `mergeSnapshots` refuses conflicting snapshots, and the Executable requires the sole presented target to match that CID before the archive. The resolution tally, frozen policy, case inventory and selection of the loser remain bespoke BabyDRO logic; the primitives do not replace them. A failed archive rolls the whole transaction back. The DRO submits the choice, with the losing credential disclosed by its anchorer for ledger visibility; the Consortium's signed member authority supplies the singleton anchorer's choice authority. The loser need not vote or submit resolution. Optional `Ruling_Follow` is only an acknowledgement, not the credential act. The script verifies the exact loser is archived while the winner and unrelated credentials remain live. This archives one exact on-ledger CIPs credential, not every credential for that name, universal name uniqueness, or external business revocation. The CIPs demo does not enforce a global namespace and other CIPs holders can still issue names. This flow requires all credential anchorers to be current Consortium signatories; the opening singleton-anchorer constraint and active-participant removal guard preserve that property for the demo. Disclosure availability is required for the opening and resolution submits. An independently archived or replaced frozen credential makes resolution fail; no on-ledger cancellation or escape exists yet. An unresolved, deadlocked or stale-credential dispute can block both participants' removal indefinitely until a separately governed terminal/cancellation path is designed. The draft CIPs PR does not mandate this governance behavior.

BabyDRO is opt-in: its implementation and demo are excluded from Concordia's `multi-package.yaml`, so `dpm build --all`, CI demo tests, and the default sandbox runner do not build or run them. Its manifests still depend on privately accessible CIPs credentials DARs. Access to those DARs is required; Concordia neither vendors them nor provides public access. The source of the name model is in-flight work on the CIPs branch `cip/canton-network-credentials-standard`, **not** a verified pinned reproducible SHA.

With the required CIPs interface and model DARs already available at the paths in `impl/daml.yaml` and `demo/daml.yaml` (the paths assume sibling `cips` and `concordia` directories), build the local prerequisites from the Concordia root. Run the individual BabyDRO builds and test from a directory outside Concordia with no ancestor `multi-package.yaml`: DPM otherwise resolves the root aggregate package list and rejects excluded packages, even with `--package-root`. For example, when the parent directory has no `multi-package.yaml`:

```sh
# From the Concordia root:
dpm build --all
CONCORDIA_ROOT="$(pwd)"
cd ..
DAML_PACKAGE="$CONCORDIA_ROOT/examples/governance/babydro/impl" dpm build
DAML_PACKAGE="$CONCORDIA_ROOT/examples/governance/babydro/demo" dpm build
DAML_PACKAGE="$CONCORDIA_ROOT/examples/governance/babydro/demo" dpm test
cd "$CONCORDIA_ROOT"
dpm script --dar examples/governance/babydro/demo/.daml/dist/cap-example-babydro-demo-0.1.0.dar --all --ide-ledger
INCLUDE_BABYDRO=1 ./scripts/sandbox-test.sh
```

The aggregate build supplies the local Concordia dependency DARs; both BabyDRO manifests also require the listed `lib/` DARs. If the private CIPs DARs are unavailable, leave BabyDRO out of the build. The opt-in sandbox run requires the built BabyDRO demo DAR and uploads it with its transitive dependencies.

The naming proposal is [canton-foundation/cips draft PR #209](https://github.com/canton-foundation/cips/pull/209), which has **no assigned CIP number**. The draft motivates dispute governance; it does not prescribe this architecture. Other demo-local choices include a distinct DRO and three founding registry operators, a fixed two-vote threshold (an off-ledger k-of-n analogue, **not cryptographic threshold signing**), party-based entitlement, a disputer-supplied case ID and reason, and explicit follow acknowledgements. Admission can enlarge the electorate only with the newcomer's consent; the threshold remains two as a local simplification. The admission invitation delegates DRO and current-member signatories' authority, the newcomer creates a target-signed consent visible to members, and a member exercises that consent to enact admission against the live motion and originating consortium. Motions left on an archived predecessor cannot enact. These delegated transitions are not fresh signatures by every current member on every successor.
