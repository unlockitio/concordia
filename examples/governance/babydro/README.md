# BabyDRO: canton.name governance walkthrough

The CIPs credentials demo owns name requests, registry-issued credentials and their lifecycle. Concordia owns only dispute governance and member entitlement. `Registry.daml` defines a `Consortium` signed by the distinct DRO Party and **every current registry member**, holding membership, a demo-local electorate policy, case-opening IDs, and proposals with current-member supporters. Genesis requires the DRO and all three founding registries; the DRO is never a member or voter and cannot propose or dispute as a registry. Admission requires two distinct current-member supporters, an invitation signed by the DRO and current members, and the newcomer's independent consent before a member finalizes the successor. Removal and policy changes require two distinct current-member supporters. The departing member is not a signatory on the removal successor, while the DRO remains its signatory and case authority. The consortium does not store ballots, rulings or follow acknowledgements. `Dispute.daml` defines separate DRO-signed `Case` and `DisputeInvitation`, jointly signed `Ballot` (DRO and voter), and DRO-signed `Ruling` contracts. The case holds the authoritative map from voter to active ballot contract ID, and the opening electorate and policy. The ruling records acknowledgements, not credential state.

Opening a case consumes `Consortium.OpenDispute` to check both disputing registries are members, create the case, and record its opening contract ID in the successor's inventory. Governance successors carry that inventory forward. The DRO's signature is carried to the case. The first consuming ballot registration records the original case ID on its successor; later case successors preserve it. The DRO (outside the registry electorate) issues a case- and voter-scoped, pre-signed invitation; that voter independently exercises `DisputeInvitation_Accept` to create a jointly signed ballot and register its ID on the case. That ballot's voter independently exercises `Ballot_Cast` or `Ballot_Withdraw` to replace their ballot and update the case inventory atomically. These voter transactions do not exercise Consortium. A contract ID by itself is **not** authority: the invitation supplies the DRO's signature to the accepting voter. This follows the `BallotInvitation`/`PrivateBallot` delegation pattern in the private-majority-vote example. LF 2.2 lacks contract keys, so the caller must use the latest returned case and ballot IDs after each consuming choice; a stale ID fails. The case validates the voter and case identity of each ballot in its inventory at registration and resolution. The case-local `caseId` is not a global uniqueness guarantee across independently opened cases.

`SnapshotAtOpen` and `CurrentMembership` are demo-local policy options. The case freezes its opening policy and eligible voter set. `Consortium_Resolve` is the only resolution entry point: it verifies that the case's opening contract ID is in its own inventory, reads its own active membership, fetches the case's inventoried ballots, and applies the case's frozen policy. No caller-supplied membership set reaches the tally. A snapshot case still counts a removed voter's ballot, including one cast after removal. A current-membership case excludes that voter's ballot at tally; a later consortium policy change does not retarget the case. Exercising a DRO-and-all-current-member-signed Consortium supplies its signatories' authority through the choice contract, but does **not** prove that the exercised contract is the unique live governance successor or that all signatories actively submitted the exercise. Direct creation requires the DRO and every named member, including for a competing Consortium; an existing jointly signed contract can carry its signatories' authority to successors under a member-controlled choice. The two-supporter registry governance threshold is therefore a business rule, not a claim of fresh unanimous transaction signatures. Only the DRO can issue a ruling, using the latest authoritative Consortium successor's membership and inventoried case. The `droTrustBoundary` script demonstrates that the DRO and two cooperating members can directly create a parallel active Consortium with copied inventory and favourable membership and resolve through it. This remains a colluding-signatories fork boundary, not an LF-level uniqueness guarantee. Similarly, the formerly enforced removal guard against removing a party in an open case or with an unfollowed ruling cannot be enforced after cases become independent; it was removed rather than retained as a misleading check. Removing a member requires the two-member threshold but is allowed while its dispute or ruling remains active.

A ruling does **not** change a CIPs name credential. Each affected registry follows by its own action on its own CIPs-owned state; the demo archives the losing registry's credential as that registry and separately records follow acknowledgements. An acknowledgement alone does not prove the external credential action: the journey checks both contract states and asserts unrelated names remain active. Governance cannot verify the existence or truth of a claimed name in CIPs state when opening a dispute; the journey demonstrates the intended ordering.

BabyDRO is opt-in: its implementation and demo are excluded from Concordia's `multi-package.yaml`, so `dpm build --all`, CI demo tests, and the default sandbox runner do not build or run them. Its manifests still depend on privately accessible CIPs credentials DARs. Access to those DARs is required; Concordia neither vendors them nor provides public access. The source of the name model is in-flight work on the CIPs branch `cip/canton-network-credentials-standard`, **not** a verified pinned reproducible SHA.

With the required CIPs interface and model DARs already available at the paths in `impl/daml.yaml` and `demo/daml.yaml` (the paths assume sibling `cips` and `concordia` directories), build the local prerequisites from the Concordia root. Run the individual BabyDRO builds and test from a directory outside Concordia with no ancestor `multi-package.yaml`: DPM otherwise resolves the root aggregate package list and rejects excluded packages, even with `--package-root`. For example, when the parent directory has no `multi-package.yaml`:

```sh
# From the Concordia root:
dpm build --all
CONCORDIA_ROOT="$(pwd)"
cd ..
DAML_PACKAGE="$CONCORDIA_ROOT/examples/governance/babydro/impl" dpm build
DAML_PACKAGE="$CONCORDIA_ROOT/examples/governance/babydro/demo" dpm build
DAML_PACKAGE="$CONCORDIA_ROOT/examples/governance/babydro/demo" dpm test
cd "$CONCORDIA_ROOT"
dpm script --dar examples/governance/babydro/demo/.daml/dist/cap-example-babydro-demo-0.1.0.dar --all --ide-ledger
INCLUDE_BABYDRO=1 ./scripts/sandbox-test.sh
```

The aggregate build supplies the local Concordia dependency DARs; both BabyDRO manifests also require the listed `lib/` DARs. If the private CIPs DARs are unavailable, leave BabyDRO out of the build. The opt-in sandbox run requires the built BabyDRO demo DAR and uploads it with its transitive dependencies.

The naming proposal is [canton-foundation/cips draft PR #209](https://github.com/canton-foundation/cips/pull/209), which has **no assigned CIP number**. The draft motivates dispute governance; it does not prescribe this architecture. Other demo-local choices include a distinct DRO and three founding registry operators, a fixed two-vote threshold (an off-ledger k-of-n analogue, **not cryptographic threshold signing**), party-based entitlement, a disputer-supplied case ID and reason, and explicit follow acknowledgements. Admission can enlarge the electorate only with the newcomer's consent; the threshold remains two as a local simplification. The admission invitation delegates DRO and current-member signatories' authority, the newcomer creates a target-signed consent visible to members, and a member exercises that consent to enact admission. A stale invitation cannot enact against an archived Consortium. These delegated transitions are not fresh signatures by every current member on every successor.
